# MarketLab — Database Design Report

*Multi-vendor marketplace and order fulfilment system — CS 202 two-person project.*

> This file is generated by `python3 tools/build_er.py` from `tools/er_model.py` and `schema.sql`. Edit those two files, not this one.

## Overview

Independent sellers list variants of catalogue products on one marketplace. A customer can buy from several sellers in one order. Each seller keeps stock in shared warehouses, so checkout reserves stock from the right seller in the right warehouse, and the order is split into one fulfilment per seller and warehouse. Packages are shipped with a carrier service and delivered by couriers. After delivery, the customer can return items and receive a refund.

The design has **67 entity sets** and **99 relationship sets**, plus one ISA hierarchy. They form one connected ER diagram.

### Notation

The diagrams follow the notation of the course slides (Ramakrishnan & Gehrke, chapter 2):

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Rectangle | Entity set. Ovals around it are its attributes; the key attribute is underlined. |
| Diamond | Relationship set. Ovals attached to a diamond are descriptive attributes of the relationship. |
| Arrow from an entity set to a diamond | Key constraint: each entity takes part in at most one relationship of that set. |
| Bold line | Total participation: each entity takes part in at least one relationship of that set. |
| Bold arrow | Both: each entity takes part in exactly one relationship of that set. |
| Bold rectangle + bold diamond | Weak entity set and its identifying relationship. The partial key has a dashed underline. |
| Triangle labelled ISA | Specialisation. Subclasses inherit the attributes and the key of the superclass. |
| Dashed box | Aggregation: the relationship inside the box is treated as an entity set, so another relationship can connect to the box. |
| Label on a line | Role name, used when the same entity set appears twice in one relationship. |

Foreign-key columns are not drawn as attributes. In the ER model a reference to another entity is a relationship, and the foreign key is only how the relational schema implements that relationship.

## The four required concepts

**Weak entities.** A weak entity can be identified only together with the key of its owner. It has total participation with a key constraint (bold arrow) in a one-to-many identifying relationship (bold diamond):

- **Order_Lines**: owner Orders, identifying relationship Contains, partial key `line_no`. Full key = (order_id, line_no).
- **Purchase_Order_Lines**: owner Purchase_Orders, identifying relationship PO_Has_Line, partial key `line_no`. Full key = (purchase_order_id, line_no).
- **Invoice_Lines**: owner Invoices, identifying relationship Invoice_Has_Line, partial key `line_no`. Full key = (invoice_id, line_no).
- **Carrier_Services**: owner Carriers, identifying relationship Offers_Service, partial key `service_code`. Full key = (carrier_id, service_code).

**ISA hierarchy.** Accounts ISA Customers, Staff, Couriers. Customers, staff and couriers share the attributes of an account (e-mail, name) and inherit its key account_id, but each has its own attributes and takes part in different relationships. Overlap is not allowed: an account has exactly one role, and triggers reject a subtype row whose account has a different role. Covering holds: role is mandatory, so every account is one of the three.

**Ternary relationship.** Stocks relates Seller_Organizations, Variants and Warehouses. A seller stocks a variant in a warehouse, with the available quantity and a reorder point. The quantity belongs to the triple: knowing that a seller sells a variant, that the seller uses a warehouse and that the warehouse holds the variant does not tell how many units of that seller's variant are in that warehouse (the Contracts argument on the Binary vs. Ternary slide).

**Aggregation.** The Stocks relationship, together with its three entity sets, is enclosed in a dashed box and treated as one entity set. Two relationships connect to the box:

- **Batch_Of**: Each batch belongs to exactly one stock position, that is, to one Stocks relationship (aggregation). A stock position consists of many batches.
- **Reserves**: An order line reserves a quantity from a stock position, that is, from a Stocks relationship (seller, variant, warehouse) treated as one object. One line may be served from several warehouses.

This is aggregation rather than a 4-ary relationship, for the reasons on the *Aggregation vs. ternary* slide. A reservation or a batch can only refer to a stock position that already exists. Reserves has its own descriptive attribute (quantity). And we can state constraints on it: each batch belongs to exactly one stock position (bold arrow).

## Design choices

- **Entity vs. attribute.** Several concepts are modelled as entity sets because an entity can have several of them, and attributes cannot be set-valued. Others are entity sets because their structure matters (see the reason under each one in Part 1).
- **Avoiding redundancy.** A coupon redemption and a return request do not store the customer again. The customer is already determined by the order, and storing it twice could let the two disagree (compare the *Entity vs. Relationship* slide).
- **Participation that SQL cannot enforce.** Total participation of the "one" side is guaranteed by the application, not by a foreign key:
  - Orders in Contains: checkout always creates at least one order line.
  - Purchase_Orders in PO_Has_Line: a purchase order is created together with its lines.
  - Invoices in Invoice_Has_Line: an invoice is issued with one line per order line.
  - Products in Variant_Of: a product is only published with at least one variant.
  - Carriers in Offers_Service: a carrier is only usable with at least one service.
- **Consistency across relationships.** Some rules involve two relationships at once, for example that a returned line belongs to the returned order. The ER diagram cannot show such a rule, because every relationship is drawn on its own. The schema enforces each one with a composite foreign key through a shared column:
  - A goods receipt only receives lines of its own purchase order (Receipt_For + Receives): `goods_receipt_lines(receipt_id, purchase_order_id)` references `goods_receipts(receipt_id, purchase_order_id)`, and `(purchase_order_id, line_no)` references the purchase-order line, so both must name the same purchase order.
  - A return request only returns lines of its own order (Return_Of + Returns): `return_lines(return_id, order_id)` references `return_requests(return_id, order_id)`, and `(order_id, line_no)` references the order line, so both must name the same order.
  - A refund is paid back from the payment of the order that was returned (Transaction_Of + Refund_For): `payment_transactions` carries `order_id` only for this: `(payment_id, order_id)` references `payments` and `(return_id, order_id)` references `return_requests`, so the payment and the return belong to the same order.
  - A stock batch is stored in a bin of its own warehouse (Batch_Of + Stored_In): `stock_batches(bin_id, warehouse_id)` references `storage_bins(bin_id, warehouse_id)`, and `warehouse_id` is also part of the batch's stock position.

## Part 2 — Relationships

### ISA: Accounts → Customers, Staff, Couriers

Customers, staff and couriers share the attributes of an account (e-mail, name) and inherit its key account_id, but each has its own attributes and takes part in different relationships. Overlap is not allowed: an account has exactly one role, and triggers reject a subtype row whose account has a different role. Covering holds: role is mandatory, so every account is one of the three.
